# Creative method — "How to create killer ad creatives" (Sergio C / ADVT Media)

## The process
1. **Research before any design tool opens.** Sources: reviews, ad/social comments, support tickets, sales-call objections, post-purchase surveys, Reddit, competitor reviews and comments under competitor ads. Extract: what the customer wants · what's stopping them · what they've already tried.
2. **One problem → one angle → one sentence.** Don't cram every selling point into one ad (that's an infomercial to someone who was watching a cat video). Everything in the creative supports the one sentence; returns/guarantees/specs go on the landing page.
3. **Format follows the message.** Static when value is obvious; video when people must see it work; plain product shots only for already-sold, high-intent audiences.
4. **Build:** hook (direct, qualifying, customer's words) → solution (plain) → show the process → visual proof for every claim. Clear beats clever.
5. **Variations:** same angle, one different thing (e.g. V1 UGC video, V2 carousel telling the same story). If one massively outperforms, you learned something.
6. **Test fairly:** creative volume must match budget ($30–100/day → focused batch). Apples-to-apples test with an impression cap (1,000; 800 on a new pixel with $50–70 CPMs) so every ad gets exposure. Don't call an ad "not working" if it never got spend; don't crown a winner because it was the only one funded.
7. **Read the journey:** Did they stop (CTR)? Did they click (small CTR → link CTR drop)? What happened after the click (carts, checkout, CVR — don't blame the creative if the page/offer/shipping is the problem)? Does it hold at higher spend? Cost of attention × conversion rate is a two-piece puzzle: 1M visitors at 0.5% conversion is never profitable.
8. **Every test is the next brief:** angle won → more on that angle · one hook crushed → that hook + 3 new bodies · demo beat review-style UGC → more demos · competitor reviews reveal a weakness → address that objection in your ad.

## Worked examples from the video
- **Walking pad** — customer words: "I sit at my desk for 8 hours and realize I barely moved" · "I want a treadmill but don't have space" · "Can't have something loud while I'm on calls". Angle: *Get more steps while you work without needing a giant treadmill taking precious space.* Hook: *"If you work from home and somehow finish the day with 800 steps when your goal was 10,000, keep listening."* Show: working at the desk → pad underneath → serious call, camera zooms out to reveal walking → pad slides away / into closet.
- **Blackout blinds** — reviews revealed nursery, RV and truck-driver use → separate angles instead of generic "blackout blinds" creative.
- **Live test, $30/day, 13 ads, cap 800** — a visually "satisfying" video: 10.9% CTR, 3+ point drop to link CTR, almost no buying (viewers didn't know what was sold). A routine/before-after demo: 6.3% CTR, < 2 point drop, bought — it showed the product, the application and the result.

## Final rule
Choose one customer problem → write the angle in one sentence → make every line of the ad prove it → change one meaningful thing at a time → let the result tell you what to make next.
